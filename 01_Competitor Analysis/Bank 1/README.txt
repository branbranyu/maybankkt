Bank 1: Standard Chartered
In-house ESG Notch-down Matrix
Standard Chartered was working on developing an internal ESG rating that is assigned to companies. The rating evaluates a company’s focus and management of ESG issues that are most material to its business. Their rating process factors in how well the company manages these ESG issues, and the ratings are updated regularly to reflect progress or deterioration against set goals.

How they determine a company's ESG rating:
1. Most common ESG metrics as per global reporting standards and frameworks.png
They researched the most common ESG metrics, according to global reporting standards. 

2. Prepare a library of ESG metrics.png
They then prepare their own list of metrics they aim to collect, via engaging external ESG experts.

3. Assign relative criticalities(weights) to identified metrics.png
They assign the  metrics based on a set of industry-specific criteria, relative to their competitors. 

4. Defining units, limits and tolerances.png
They experimented with three methods of defining units, limits and tolerances for different types of clients.

5. Mechanism for scoring and integration with credit decision-making.png
They then factor in the client's ESG performance by using a Notch-Down matrix to form a combined internal score. This approach allows them to better assess the non-financial risks associated with sustainable investing and to monitor companies' involvement in ESG controversies.
